Playbook · Program Directors

Extend the project before it ends.

The PD's job is to facilitate the discovery and pitch of the next impactful initiative, whether that's a new phase of the current work or something net new, early enough that the client signs before the current SOW closes.

The thesis

Build on the momentum: iterate, expand, or strategize what's next.

The expansion usually happens under one of three premises: continue iterating on what we built to make it better, apply AI or technology elsewhere in the business to keep transforming it, or strategize on where to apply it next when the answer isn't yet obvious. The PD's job is to work with the team to figure out which one (or which combination) is right, and put it in front of the client before the current SOW closes.

The High-level Playbook

Run the current engagement well

Client happy. Team performing. Work moving the metric in the SOW. Everything else compounds from this, there is no expansion conversation if Phase 1 isn't landing.

Run continual discovery

Throughout the project, not at the end, surface what else matters to the client. Coach the PM to listen for and shape what's next, not just transcribe it. Adjacent pains, things "on the back burner," what the client says is coming. Probe in your own weekly check-ins. Capture every signal.

Strategize the next chapter early

At least 2 weeks before the SOW closes, run a strategy session with BD, PM, and key ICs. Decide what to pitch, in what format, and who's in the room.

Identify the pain

You're in the room every week. The client trusts you. Use that access to surface what they haven't told you yet, and what other departments are quietly carrying. The expansion pitch comes from naming a real pain, not from inventing a project.

Most pain inside a client org is one of three flavors: the metric the buyer is being measured on that won't move, the workaround a team is paying for in time and headcount, or the adjacent problem a different department keeps bringing up but no one owns. Listen for all three. Frame your questions as "I want to make sure we solve the right problem next" so the client gives you the real answer instead of the polished one.

Make smart assumptions and inferences. You've been in the weekly check-ins, you've seen the team's data, and you've watched what they work around. A specific question rooted in what you've learned lands ten times harder than a generic one because it proves you've been paying attention. The split below gives you both modes: inference-driven questions when you're pitching an extension to the current project, and broader questions when you're hunting for a brand-new initiative.

The most useful pain is one with a number attached, an owner who feels it, and a deadline that makes it urgent. Get those three things on every pain you hear, and the next pitch writes itself.

For expanding the current project

Lead with inference. Use the team's data, the patterns you've seen, the friction you've noticed. The client should hear "they've been watching" in every question.

  • "Now that [feature we shipped] is live, the natural next bottleneck looks like [your inference]. Where's that on your priority list?"
  • "We noticed [specific pattern in the data] during the build. Is that worth investigating in a phase 2?"
  • "Earlier in the project you mentioned [back-burner thing]. Has it gotten more painful since we started, or less?"
  • "The same data spine / model / surface we built for [X] could power [Y]. Which one moves the bigger metric for you next quarter?"
  • "Your team has been working around [friction we saw]. Is that worth solving in the next phase, or is it small enough to live with?"

For a brand-new initiative

Stay general until they show you the pain. If they're cagey on specifics, fall back on how they're thinking about AI more broadly, that almost always opens a door.

  • "If you could fix one thing in the business in the next 6 months, what would it be?"
  • "What are your top 3 business objectives this year? Which is hardest to move?"
  • "Where in the company are you spending the most on workarounds today?"
  • "Which of your peers (CFO, COO, CTO, CMO) is dealing with the biggest fire right now?"
  • "How are you thinking about AI more broadly, what bets are you making, what are you holding off on, and where do you want help thinking it through?"

Three potential paths to extensions

Phase 2 / scope extension

The client already knows what they want next. Our value: estimate it, plan it, scope a clean SOW the same team picks up on day one. Fastest path to continuation. One-pager usually lands it.

Strategy → implementation

The client knows something else needs solving but isn't sure what. Lead with a $25K strategy engagement to figure out where AI fits next, then transition the same team into the build. Two SOWs, one continuous engagement.

Land and expand

An adjacent problem the team identified during delivery. Pitch it as a parallel build or as the next priority. Lean hard on what the client said mattered during the first engagement, that's what makes it land instead of feeling like a sale.

The cadence

Throughout the project

Continual discovery captured weekly. PD coaches PM to listen and log. PD probes during weekly check-ins. Build a running list of discovered opportunities, the highest-conversion expansion conversations are rooted in things the client already said matters.

~4 weeks before project end

Signal to the client that an extension conversation is coming. Brief the BD lead informally on what you're seeing in discovery. Confirm capacity for the team to continue.

At least 2 weeks before project end

Internal strategy session with PD, BD lead, PM, and key ICs. Decide what to pitch, the format (deck or one-pager, team's call), and who presents (PD alone, BD & PD, PD & PM, full team, etc.).

Before project end

Pitch the client. Land the next SOW so the same team continues without a gap. If the client needs more time to decide, propose a small bridging engagement so context stays warm and the team doesn't roll off.

What to listen for during the project

The expansion pitch writes itself when you've been logging signals all along. Train your PM to capture these, and ask about them yourself in weekly check-ins, and ICs.

  • "This is great, but what we really need is..."
  • "On the back burner..." / "We've been meaning to..."
  • "Once this is done, we want to..."
  • "The board / our investors are asking about..."
  • "I wish someone could just..."
  • Adjacent metrics moving in the wrong direction
  • Tools or vendors the client is frustrated with
  • New roles being hired, every new hire is a signal of where the org is going next
The internal strategy session

Two weeks out, get the band in the room.

Who: PD + BD lead + PM + key ICs.
Inputs: the running discovery list, current metrics, client appetite signals, capacity available.
Outputs: the pitch (Phase 2, strategy, or land-and-expand), the format (deck or one-pager), the room (PD alone, BD & PD, PD & PM, full team, etc.), and a draft SOW skeleton.

Two incentives for the client

Two reasons the client should sign the next SOW now, before the current one closes.

1. Same team continues

The same PM, AI engineers, and ML scientist stay on as long as the engagement continues. The deep context they've earned is the asset, and it transfers to the next phase for free. A pause could mean the team rolls onto another engagement and the next conversation starts cold.

2. Possible discount for a longer-term partnership

If the client commits to a longer-term partnership (typically 6 to 12 months, more if needed), we may be able to discount rates. This requires CTO / CFO approval and is handled on a case-by-case basis, so loop them in before you put a number on the table.

The pitch

Lead with what they told you

The strongest opener: "Three months ago you said X was important. Here's what shipping it looks like." Discovery insights make the conversation feel like a continuation, not a sale.

Make the same-team promise explicit

"The same PM, AI engineers, and ML scientist you've been working with stay on, as long as we keep going. The deep context they've earned is yours to keep. A pause means we lose them." That promise alone often closes the room.

Match the format to the lift

Phase 2 obvious to both sides? One-pager. New direction or new strategy? Slide deck with the discovery insights, the option set, and the recommendation. The team decides, same call as the staffing call.

Your weekly checklist

  • Run the current week well, protect the current SOW above all else
  • Capture at least one new discovery insight (your own or from the PM)
  • Track distance to project end, count down from 4 weeks
  • 4 weeks out: brief BD lead informally, signal the client
  • 2 weeks out: schedule and run the internal strategy session
  • Before project end: pitch the client and secure the next SOW
