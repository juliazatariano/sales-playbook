Common objections (Education)

The handful you'll hear across most edtech conversations. Pull from this; tailor to the specific offer in play.

AI avatars and voice agents feel uncanny. Parents and learners notice.

Avatar and voice design is curriculum-led, not entertainment-led. Latency, prosody, and turn-taking are the design surface, Pimsleur ships at sub-500ms, which is why 90% of users said they felt more confident speaking. Stilted is a build problem, not a category problem.

Won't this replace teachers?

At Alpha, teachers became coaches. The AI handles deliberate practice; the teacher handles motivation, social-emotional development, and edge cases. Same staffing model, different work, better outcomes.

Districts and parents have safety / privacy concerns about AI with kids.

FERPA compliance, content guardrails, audit logs, district admin controls, and parent dashboards are built in. Every session is reviewable. Alpha and Subject.com both ship under exactly these constraints.

AI can't grade essays well enough, we tried Turnitin / GPT-based graders.

Generic graders don't know your rubric, your assignment, or your student. We train on yours; rubric-alignment rate and hallucination rate are both in the SOW. Teacher-in-the-loop on high-variance cases, human-in-the-loop is the architecture, not a fallback.

Students will use this to cheat.

The student-facing co-pilot is designed for the student's process, not the answer. Watermarking and process telemetry handle integrity.
