The AE point of view

Everyone says they care about safety. Almost no one funds the research to prove it. We do.

The field has converged on a handful of approaches — RLHF, Constitutional AI, output filters — and called it a day. But these methods are patches, not solutions. Contemporary models can produce harmful outputs, hide dangerous backdoors, and deceive evaluators. The researchers closest to the problem know this. In a survey of active alignment researchers, fewer than one in ten believed current techniques would solve alignment before AGI.

AE's approach is different: pursue neglected directions, publish the work, and bring in collaborators who share the urgency — Anthropic, DARPA, Redwood Research, Princeton, the National Security Council. We don't do this for positioning. We do it because we think timelines are short and the mainstream research agenda has gaps that nobody else is filling.

Research AI Labs · Safety Teams · Foundations

Self-Other Overlap

Reducing LLM deception by training models to more accurately represent the perspectives of others. If a model can't distinguish its own state from another agent's, deception becomes structurally harder. Peer-reviewed, in active development.

Relevant when"our model says one thing to evaluators and another in deployment"

+

When this comes up

  • A lab is seeing sycophancy or evaluation gaming they can't explain
  • A safety team suspects their model behaves differently under evaluation than in production
  • A foundation wants to fund neglected deception-reduction research

What the research addresses

  • Structural deception: models that represent their own state differently depending on audience
  • Self-other overlap as a mechanism for making deception harder at the architecture level
  • Evaluation robustness: methods that hold up when the model knows it's being evaluated
Research AI Labs · Policy Bodies · Foundations

Prosociality in Language Models

Reverse-engineering what drives cooperative, beneficial behavior in LLMs. Rather than patching outputs, we investigate the internal dynamics that produce genuinely prosocial responses — and how to make them more robust.

Relevant when"we want models that are helpful for the right reasons, not because they're told to be"

+

When this comes up

  • A lab wants to understand why their model is helpful, not just confirm that it is
  • A policy body wants mechanistic evidence for why certain training approaches produce safer behavior
  • A foundation is funding interpretability or value-learning work

What the research addresses

  • Internal representations that correlate with cooperative vs. misaligned behavior
  • Whether prosociality can be instilled mechanistically, or only approximated through training incentives
  • Robustness of prosocial behavior across distribution shift and adversarial prompting

Common objections (Alignment)

What you'll hear — and how to respond.

We already have a safety team / red-team / responsible AI policy.

Internal safety teams are valuable, but they're inside the same incentive structure as the product. We're independent. We publish the results whether they're flattering or not. That's a different kind of credibility than an internal review.

Is this just AI ethics consulting with a research veneer?

No. Our researchers publish in peer-reviewed venues on mechanistic questions — self-other overlap, prosociality in LLMs, physics-based constraints. This isn't policy or principles work. It's technical alignment research that happens to be available to clients who want it applied to their systems.

Why does an AI services firm do alignment research?

Because we're bootstrapped with no outside investors, so no one tells us to stop. And because the team that understands alignment at a mechanistic level builds better AI systems. The research and the applied work make each other sharper. That's not incidental — it's why we structured the company this way.

Is this ready for production use, or is it just research?

Both. The research programs are academic and long-horizon. The safety consulting and strategy engagements are production-focused — they deliver working systems and concrete recommendations, not white papers.
